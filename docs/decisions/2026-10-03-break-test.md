# Break test, 3 Oct 2026

An adversarial test of everything built so far (code, inputs, evaluation, process), run by trying to make it
fail rather than to confirm it, plus an independent read-only review by a second agent. Evidence:
`results/break_test/report.txt` (regenerated by `scripts/break_test/`).

**Result: nothing broke the Gate 2 claim, but the claim is smaller and more specific than we had written.**
The registered numbers still stand as run. A corrected re-run (Amendment 2) is the next step and has **not**
been executed yet.

## What held

- **Reproducible.** A fresh clone installs from scratch, passes the suite and regenerates the committed audit,
  the full test-split results and the dev rows **bit for bit**. The first run (commit `b733db2`) also
  regenerates identically.
- **No leakage.** On four real patients, scrambling every hidden reading of both sensors leaves the estimate,
  band, fit and meal-clock offset unchanged; cutting the recording 1.5 days after the split leaves earlier
  estimates unchanged; moving one calibration reading moves the estimate (the test can detect a change).
  The reviewer read the code for indirect paths (interpolation near the split, initial state, shapes, seeds)
  and found none.
- **P1 is robust.** On the 20 test patients: leave-one-patient-out worst p = 6.3e-05; median difference -1.48
  mg/dL, 95 % bootstrap CI [-3.49, -0.79]; mean RMSE change -7.2 % [-9.9, -4.5]; every group and exclusion
  subset keeps the sign; with day 1 of every recording dropped (a different calibration window), 22.0 vs 25.1
  mg/dL, better in 84 %, p = 4e-04; scored against the Dexcom, which was never used in the fit, 26.9 vs 27.3,
  better in 90 %, p = 1.8e-05.

## What the claim should say

1. **The comparator is weak.** At k = 5 the registered average day is no better than the patient's mean. A
   control with no physiology (half average day, half mean) scores 22.55 vs Chhaya 22.54 mg/dL; Chhaya beats it
   in 70 % of test patients, median 0.39 mg/dL, p = 0.03 (dev: 0.71). Chhaya beats the raw average day by 1.5;
   it beats the control by about 0.4 to 0.7. **The honest effect is 0.4 to 0.7 mg/dL against a fair control.**
2. **The meal log is what carries it.** Replacing the logged meals with none, with the same meals 6 hours late,
   or with each patient's own habitual meals makes the estimate worse by 1.2 to 2.0 mg/dL (all p < 0.001).
   On hidden days with 3 or more logged meals Chhaya beats the control in 86 % of patient-days (21.7 vs 24.3);
   on the 9 test patient-days with no meal logged it is worse (25.3 vs 22.9). Restricting to days with two or
   more logged meals: Chhaya vs control median -1.58 mg/dL, better in 75 %, p = 0.005.
3. **The physiology alone does not beat the control** (median -0.9, p = 0.55 on days with a log). The blend
   works because the two halves err differently.
4. **Activity adds nothing measurable.** Replacing the band with rest after the split changes the test RMSE
   by 0.03 mg/dL.
5. **It does not track which day is unusual.** The advantage is the same on typical and unusual days
   (r = 0.02 with how unusual the day's carbohydrate was). Per-meal, the predicted two-hour rise correlates
   with the observed rise at 0.42 pooled and 0.17 per patient on test patients (average day 0.36 and 0.07).
6. **It is a five-day effect.** The advantage holds on each of the five days after the sensor comes off
   (-0.7 to -2.8 mg/dL, 63 to 93 % of patients); on day six (23 patients) it reverses (+0.9).
7. **Time in range is not a strength.** Chhaya's time-in-range error is 4.7 points; the patient's own average
   day gets 3.4 and "same as the sensor fortnight" gets 4.9, so P2 cannot discriminate (a constant passes it).
   The probability-based time in range, which uses the band, is worse: 17 points. Do not claim time-in-range
   estimation.
8. **Correction to what we said earlier.** We reported the two sensors as disagreeing by about 39 mg/dL "noise"
   and said Chhaya's error sits inside it. Where both sensors recorded, they differ by a **systematic offset
   of 32 mg/dL (the Dexcom reads higher) with a random spread of only 13 mg/dL**, and the RMSE is 35. So
   Chhaya's 22 mg/dL is not inside random sensor noise. The earlier figure (39.5) was also inflated by comparing
   against a flat extrapolation of the Dexcom after it stopped recording.
9. **Effect size.** Chhaya's RMSE is 0.97 of the standard deviation of the hidden glucose; the average day's is
   1.04. Minute by minute it explains very little variance; its value is modest and consistent, not large.

## Defects found and fixed (each with a failing test first; 128 tests pass)

## The claim to quote

On 20 held-out patients, 5 days after the sensor comes off, Chhaya's estimate is 1.5 mg/dL (7 %) closer to the
hidden sensor than the patient's own average day (p = 3e-05), and about 0.4 mg/dL closer than a simple control
that uses no meals (p = 0.03). The gain comes from the meal log, lasts about five days, and comes with no
evidence yet that it tracks unusual days. It is a modest, consistent effect on 45 US participants, not a
replacement for a sensor.
