## What it means

- With 3 days of sensor the twin already beats the average day; the average day needs more days to catch up.
  That is a real, publishable-as-measured property, but it is not the pre-registered primary claim.
- The twin does **not improve with more calibration days** (22.9, 23.1, 22.2) while the average day does
  (25.8, 23.1, 22.0). The twin is limited by structure, not by data.

## Diagnosis (25 development patients only)

1. **In-sample misfit is already large**: calibration residual 15 mg/dL in healthy, 30 in T2D. The twin
   reproduces about half of the observed variability (SD 27 vs 42 mg/dL in T2D).
2. **The average meal response is right in size, slightly late**: T2D +47 observed vs +47 twin, but the twin
   peaks at 75 min (observed 45 to 60) and its tail is too long (+10 vs +2 at 4 to 5 hours).
3. **Time of day is mis-specified**: in T2D the twin is 13 mg/dL too high at 03:00 to 06:00 and 11 to 13 too
   low from 06:00 to 12:00, then 13 too high after noon. One fixed-phase cosine on hepatic output cannot
   represent this. The average-day baseline gets it for free.
4. **31 % of glucose rises of 40 mg/dL or more have no logged meal nearby** (40 % in T2D). The twin cannot
   know about unlogged eating; the average day absorbs the habitual part of it.
5. **The lab fasting-glucose prior is too tight for a Libre**: fitted basal glucose sits 1.2 to 2.1 prior SDs
   below the lab value in every group, one patient at the bound. The Libre reads low (healthy participants
   spend 22.8 % of time below 70 mg/dL).

## Two loader defects found while diagnosing (inputs were wrong, independent of the model)

- **`Amount Consumed` has two unit conventions.** 9 files (016 to 023, 030) record 1.0 for a whole meal; the
  loader assumed percent and scaled their meals to 1 % of the true carbohydrate. Those participants were
  effectively run with no meals. Values above a whole meal (2 to 9, or 200 to 900) are item counts for
  self-chosen dinners and snacks, not multipliers: their calories, carbohydrate and 2-hour glucose rise match
  ordinary meals (451 vs 448 kcal, 49 vs 52 g, 32 vs 33 mg/dL).
- **Meal clocks are an hour early in 4 files** (005, 006, 008, 030): glucose does not rise after the logged
  time (mean 60-minute change -19 to +5 mg/dL) but rises 31 to 45 mg/dL one hour later.

Disclosure: finding these involved looking at the meal columns and a model-free meal-to-rise lag statistic for
all 45 files, including test patients. No model output on test patients was used to choose a fix.

## Plan for the iteration (each change test-first; chosen on dev patients, claimed on test patients)

1. Fix the two loader defects by rule, not by patient: detect the `Amount Consumed` unit per file; estimate a
   per-patient meal-clock offset from that patient's calibration window only.
2. Add a per-patient time-of-day profile learned from calibration residuals (shrunk toward zero), so the twin
   nests the average-day baseline instead of competing with it.
3. Widen the basal-glucose prior and learn the sensor-vs-lab offset on dev patients.
4. Robust loss, so unlogged meals do not distort the fitted parameters.
5. Faster absorption and shorter tail if dev patients still show the 15 to 30 minute lag after 1 to 4.

The confirmatory claim after these changes is made on the 20 test patients only, against the same bars.
If it still misses on 8 Oct, the headline changes as planned.
