# Band recalibration, 8 Oct 2026

**No verdict: descriptive, no bar. The recalibration did not transfer to held-out patients, so it is not
used.** The 80 % band stays as Gate 2 scored it: calibrated on average, not per patient.

## What this is

Registered in `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`, Amendment 3, "Descriptive, no bar" ("Band"); how it is computed, the
rule that chooses the design and the rule for whether the result is used are in the note on the descriptive
outputs and its addendum, written before any test patient was read.

- The band's two half-widths about the estimate are multiplied by one factor, or by one factor per day since
  the sensor. The factor and the choice between the two come from the 25 CGMacros development patients at
  k = 5; the 19 test participants only report.
- Development: `python -m chhaya.eval.calibrate` (`results/calibrate/cgmacros-dev/`). The design was committed
  in `band.json` (content hash `d157b392...`) before the test pass, which refuses any other file.
- Test: `python -m chhaya.eval.calibrate --confirm`, run once (`results/calibrate/cgmacros/`). Provenance:
  commit `1f50bd4`, no uncommitted changes under `src/`. The traces it reads gave back the Gate 2 run's error
  and coverage for every recording, and "before" below equals the coverage Gate 2 committed (0.835, range
  0.60 to 0.985).

## The design chosen on development patients

One factor, **0.78**: on development patients the band was too wide (mean coverage 0.874, 14 of 25 patients
within 70 to 90 %).

| On development patients left out of the fit | One factor | One factor per day (days 1 to 5) |
|---|---|---|
| Distance of each day's coverage from 80 %, mean over days | 3.0 points | 0.2 points |
| Patients within 70 to 90 % | 17 of 25 | 14 of 25 |

The factor per day (0.77, 0.74, 0.70, 0.71, 0.69) brought each day closer to target but left fewer patients
within 70 to 90 %, which is the number the registration reports. By the rule fixed beforehand it was not
chosen.

times 0.78: 0.80, 0.74, 0.82, 0.73, 0.74.

## Whether the recalibrated band is used

**No.** The rule registered beforehand: use it only if, on test participants at k = 5, mean coverage is no
further from 80 % than before **and** no fewer participants fall within 70 to 90 %. Before, the band covered
0.835, which is 3.5 points above target. After, 0.757, which is 4.3 points below. It is further from target,
so the band is shown as Gate 2 scored it. One more participant falls within 70 to 90 % (11 against 10); the
rule needs both conditions.

## What it means

- **The band is calibrated on average and not per patient, and one factor does not fix that.** On held-out
  participants it covers 83.5 % of hidden readings where 80 % is the target, and 60 % to 98.5 % from one
  participant to another. Ten of 19 fall within 70 to 90 %.
- **The factor did not transfer because the two halves of the cohort differ**, not because the method is
  subtle: the band was 7.4 points too wide on development patients and 3.5 on test participants. A factor
  fitted on 25 people over-corrects 19 others. A per-patient spread is what is miscalibrated, and a
  cohort-wide factor cannot reach it.
- **Coverage does not fall with days since the sensor** on the days most participants reach (expiry record).
  The band does not need to widen with time inside a week.

## What a reader should weigh

- 25 development and 19 test participants, a third of them with type 2 diabetes.
- The split between the two is by a hash of the patient identifier. Nothing was chosen to make them differ.
- The comparison is at k = 5, the Gate 2 setting. At k = 3 the factor also lowers the number of participants
  within 70 to 90 % (14 to 12).
- "Did not transfer" is a statement about this factor and these participants. A per-patient recalibration
  (for example from each patient's own calibration residuals) was not registered and was not tried.

## Consequences for the product

- The dashboard draws the band exactly as Gate 2 scored it, with this sentence beside it: "80 % band; on
  held-out participants it held 83.5 % of readings on average and between 60 % and 98.5 % for an individual."
- `chhaya.eval.calibrate.rescale` is not called by the artifact build.
- The evidence screen shows this result with the registered rule.

## The claim to quote

On 19 held-out participants the 80 % band held 83.5 % of hidden readings on average and between 60 % and
98.5 % for an individual, with 10 of 19 participants within 70 to 90 %. A recalibration factor chosen on
development patients (0.78) moved the average to 75.7 %, further from 80 % than before, so it is not used:
the band is calibrated on average, not per patient.
