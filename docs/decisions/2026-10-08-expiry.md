# Expiry: how long one sensor report stays true, 8 Oct 2026

**No verdict: descriptive, no bar.** Three outputs on held-out patients, each run once. **These data do not
give a number of days.** Within about ten days the report's mean drifted by about 1 mg/dL per day, downward,
in supervised care with treatment being adjusted, and no ageing was detected over a week in free-living
participants. Beyond that there are nine repeat wears of eight patients, and the four whose mean had moved all
had a change of treatment in the files.

Throughout, "the report" means **mean sensor glucose only**. Time below range and variability were not
assessed, and a three-day or five-day report stands in for a fourteen-day one.

Reading the tables. "Distance" is how far a later day's own mean lies from the mean of the calibration days,
a median over patients. "Day 0" is the same thing inside the wear: one calibration day against the other
calibration days, which is what an unaged report looks like. A day is a 24-hour block from the moment the
sensor is hidden. The sensor is both the report and the yardstick.

## What it means for "how long the report stays true"

- **These data do not give a number of days.** Over at most eleven days, the distance of a day's mean from
  the report grew by about 1 mg/dL per day in supervised care, downward, and was not detectably different
  from an ordinary day inside the wear until about the fifth day.
- **Over seven days no ageing of the mean was detected in 19 free-living participants** (7 with type 2
  diabetes; medication not recorded).
- **Beyond eleven days there are nine later wears of eight patients.** The later wears that had moved were
  those with a treatment change in the files (4 of 5), not those with the longest gaps. The four with no
  treatment change in the files had not moved by more than 20 mg/dL (two within three days, two at 33 and 69
  days).
- **What these data cannot show.** No patient was observed whose glucose rose, and none was followed in
  ordinary outpatient care. So they cannot show that a report holds while treatment is unchanged, and they
  say nothing about a patient who worsens with no change in the record.

This is an observation about records. It is not a rule for when a patient should wear a sensor.

## What it means for the Gate 2 sentence

The Gate 2 record (`2026-10-04-gate2-corrected.md`) says the meal log's gain "lasts about five days". That
came from the break test at five days of calibration, where the sixth hidden day is the eleventh day of the
wear. With three days of calibration the median favours the twin on each of the seven days that most
participants reach, and the reversal sits at the same day of the wear. **"Lasts about five days" is wrong as
worded.** The Gate 2 record is not edited; from now on the clause reads:

> The gain comes from the meal log and is small. It was not seen to fade with days since the sensor: with
> three days of calibration the median favours the twin over its control on each of the seven following days
> (0.4 to 2.0 mg/dL; the interval excludes zero on three of them; 19 held-out participants), and it reverses
> only on the last days of the recording, which fewer than half of the participants reach.

`docs/PROJECT_GUIDE.md` and `docs/PROGRESS.md` carry the corrected clause with a pointer to this record.

## What a reader should weigh

- **Neither cohort is the use case.** The product is for an outpatient with type 2 diabetes on stable
  therapy, seen weeks later. Shanghai is supervised care with treatment being adjusted (65 of 109 recordings
  on insulin); CGMacros is free-living, but 12 of its 19 test participants do not have type 2 diabetes and
  its medication is not recorded. There is no Indian cohort.
- **"The report" is mean glucose only.** Time below range and variability were not assessed.
- A three-day or five-day report stands in for a fourteen-day one. A shorter report is noisier, so the
  distance inside the wear and afterwards are both larger than a real report's would be.
- Day 0 is built from k - 1 days where a later day is read against k, so it slightly overstates the distance
  of an unaged report. That makes "not further than day 0" a little easier to find.
- The same sensor is both the report and the yardstick. Drift of the sensor over its own wear and its
  first-day behaviour cannot be separated from the patient's physiology.
- Later days hold fewer patients, and not a random subset. The slope inside each patient is the figure to
  trust; the by-day medians beyond the days 80 % reach are not.
- Hidden windows end at eleven days. Beyond that there are eight patients.
- Illness, steroids, adherence, diet and weight move glucose with no change of treatment in a record.
- In the case series, treatment is what the files list; a changed agents list is counted as a change
  whatever was changed. The link between treatment change and movement is four later wears in three
  patients, all downward.
- Two wears are two sensors. A difference between them includes the difference between the sensors, which
  was not measured.

## Consequences for the product

- **Patient screen:** "days since the sensor" is shown as a fact, with no threshold and no per-day figure
  beside it. The drift is on the Evidence screen and in a note: "Measured over at most 11 days: about
  1 mg/dL per day, downward, in supervised care with treatment being adjusted; none detected over 7 days in
  free-living participants (7 of 19 with type 2 diabetes). Beyond 11 days: eight patients. A cohort figure,
  not this patient's; not to be multiplied by the days shown."
- **A recorded treatment change** is shown as: "Treatment changed since the sensor. In a case series the
  sensor mean had moved by more than 20 mg/dL in 4 of 5 repeat wears after a change (3 of 4 patients) and in
  0 of 4 without. No recorded change does not mean the report still holds."
- **Clinic list:** no default ranking by the treatment flag. "Treatment changed since the sensor (from the
  record)" and "days since the sensor" are two sortable columns; "not recorded" is its own state, never shown
  as "unchanged". Under the list: "This describes how old the sensor report is, not how unwell a patient is
  or who should be seen first. A patient with no recorded change may still have changed."
- **Reveal figure:** the twin against its control can be shown for seven days at k = 3. The last days of a
  recording are marked, not hidden.
- **Limits banner:** the design says "validated on Chinese and US cohorts in supervised care". CGMacros is
  free-living and these outputs are descriptive. The banner now reads "evaluated on a Chinese cohort in
  supervised care and a US free-living cohort" (design spec corrected in this commit).
