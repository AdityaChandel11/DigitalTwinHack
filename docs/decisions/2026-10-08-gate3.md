## Also reported, as registered

- **Sensor on.** AUPRC 0.760. The fused sensor-off model keeps 0.55 of the sensor-on gain over prevalence
  (no interval was registered for this share).
- **Alerts.** At the threshold that gives 80 % sensitivity on development meals, test sensitivity is 86 % with
  8.9 false alerts per patient-week, against 21.4 eligible meals per patient-week. That is an alert at about
  two in three meals that are not followed by an excursion. It is not usable as an alert.
- **Net benefit**, fused model against alerting at every meal: 0.180 against 0.131 at threshold 0.3, 0.072
  against -0.217 at 0.5, -0.027 against -1.028 at 0.7. At 0.7 it is below never alerting. The fused
  probabilities are overconfident (slope 0.48), so fixed probability thresholds flatter nobody.
- **Timing.** The first reading above 180 comes a median of 30 minutes after the meal (472 meals).
- **Above 250 mg/dL** (9.9 % of meals): fused 0.153, sensor history 0.223, plain personal rate 0.204, sensor on
  0.303. Fused minus sensor history: -0.069 (-0.159 to 0.007). The personal rate and the history features in

- **Meals that start at or below 180** (1,041 meals, 30.7 % with the event): fused 0.493, sensor history
  0.566, fingersticks 0.560, sensor on 0.567. Here the live sensor adds nothing over the sensor week. Most of
  the sensor-on advantage in the primary analysis comes from the 164 meals that start already above 180.
- **LightGBM at library defaults**, fused inputs: AUPRC 0.623, AUROC 0.761, slope 0.47, within-patient AUROC
  0.566. A different learner does not change the picture. No test of the difference was registered.
- **Subgroups**, fused model, AUROC (AUPRC is not comparable across groups with different event rates): on

## What it means

- **Without the sensor, the excursion is partly predictable, and the simplest predictor is as good as any.**
  Every sensor-off arm sits between 0.57 and 0.64 AUPRC against a prevalence of 0.39. The patient's own
  excursion rate from the sensor week, with no model, is in that range.
- **The sensor-off models know who spikes, not when.** Within a patient the fused model's AUROC is 0.62 and
  the fingerstick arm's is 0.57; the sensor-on arm's is 0.78.
- **Fusion did not beat the best single stream.** By point estimate it was 0.046 below fingersticks only and
  0.030 below sensor history only; neither interval excludes zero.
- **The development run said the same thing first.** Cross-validated on development patients: fused 0.618,
  sensor history 0.644, difference -0.025 (-0.070 to 0.015). Nothing was changed in response.
- **A likely reason, not a tested one.** The record arm fits 13 patient-level inputs on 49 patients. It is
  the least accurate arm and badly overconfident (slope 0.31 in development, 0.52 on test), and the fused
  model inherits that. This is a reading of the tables, made after the result.

- Added because the registration lists them and the plan had not coded them: the LightGBM sensitivity run and
  the minutes from meal to the first reading above 180.

## Limits specific to this experiment

- The insulin flag reads the record's agents list, as registered. In development data it misses 5 of 29
  recordings in which insulin was dosed.
- A fingerstick stamped at the same minute as the meal is not used, as registered. Shanghai fingersticks are
  taken at fixed times that often coincide with meals, so the fingerstick and fused arms are lower bounds.
- False alerts are counted at eligible meals only.
- Supervised hospital care, one country, at most about ten hidden days.

## Consequences for the product

- **Stream switch (M4, 4.2):** shows these measured numbers. It cannot be presented as fusion winning.
- **Probabilities:** risk bands only (M3 missed).
- **No meal alert.** The alert figures above rule it out.
- **Second fusion test:** the record as the twin's prior (Plan 3, section P) is still to be read.
- **Open, for the team lead:** the headline says Chhaya "predicts post-meal excursions without the sensor".
  What the data supports is narrower: the sensor week already tells you which patients spike after meals, and
  nothing we added without the sensor improves on that. The headline clause should be reworded or dropped.

## The claim to quote

On 47 held-out Shanghai patients (1,205 meals, 39 % followed by an excursion above 180 mg/dL), a model fusing
the record, the sensor week and fingersticks predicted the excursion at meal time with AUPRC 0.60, which was
not better than any single stream (fingersticks only: 0.64) nor than the patient's own excursion rate from the
sensor week (0.59; difference 0.01, 95 % interval -0.09 to 0.14); with the sensor on, the same model reaches
0.76.
